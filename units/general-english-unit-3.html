Common writing errors

Small grammar choices can change how clear a sentence feels. This unit focuses on agreement, pronouns, articles, prepositions, modifiers and repetition, then gives a calm way to proofread.

PICTURE IT

Proofreading is a small detective game. Look for the clue that makes a sentence fit.

Proofreading is like checking a drawing in layers: first make sure the picture says what you mean, then inspect the small lines and labels.

BY THE END, YOU CAN

  • Match subjects and verbs in number.
  • Use pronouns and articles with clear reference.
  • Find misplaced modifiers, wrong word forms and unnecessary repetition.

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Find the real subject before choosing the verb

A singular subject takes a singular verb in the relevant tense: “Each student has submitted the work.” Words between subject and verb can distract, so locate the true subject first. In “The box of pencils is here,” box is the subject, not pencils.

02

Pronouns and articles guide the reader

A pronoun should match its reference and grammatical role: “The teacher praised them,” where them receives the action. A and an depend on the following sound; the signals an item the reader can identify. “A university” begins with a consonant sound; “an hour” begins with a vowel sound. Prepositions often follow common patterns.

03

Put describing words beside what they describe

A misplaced modifier can confuse: “Walking to class, the rain soaked Maya” makes it sound as if the rain was walking. Place the modifier next to the person or thing it describes. Remove redundant pairs such as “advance planning”; planning already means preparation. Proofread first for meaning, then agreement, word choice and punctuation.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Correct: “Each of the students have submitted there assignment.”

  1. Find the subject: each is singular, so use has.
  2. Choose the possessive their for an assignment belonging to the student.
  3. A clear revision is: “Each student has submitted their assignment.” In formal agreement exercises, “an assignment” is also clear.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • The noun nearest the verb is not always the subject.
  • There refers to a place; their shows belonging; they're means they are.
  • Spell-check may accept the wrong homophone, so read for meaning.

CORE IDEAS TO REMEMBER
01

A singular subject takes a singular verb: Each student has submitted the work.

02

A pronoun should match its reference and grammatical role: The teacher praised them, not they.

03

Use a, an and the according to sound, specificity and whether the reader knows which item is meant.

04

A misplaced modifier sits too far from the word it describes.

05

Avoid redundant pairs such as advance planning when planning already means preparation in advance.

IMPORTANT QUESTIONS · TAP TO REVEAL MODEL ANSWERS
QUESTION 1Correct and explain: Each of the students have submitted their assignment.+

Correct: Each of the students has submitted an assignment. The subject is each, which is singular, so the verb is has. In formal agreement exercises, an assignment is the clearest singular noun phrase. In ordinary modern usage, singular they is also common when referring to a person whose gender is unknown, but it does not change the verb: Each student has submitted their assignment.

QUESTION 2Correct: She advised them to keep doing there best in future.+

Correct: She advised them to keep doing their best in the future. Their is a possessive determiner meaning belonging to them; there refers to a place. The phrase in the future is the usual form. Proofreading should check homophones as well as grammar, because spell-check may accept both there and their.
