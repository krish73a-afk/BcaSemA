🪷 INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM · UNIT III

Mathematics & astronomy

This unit surveys mathematical and astronomical practices through texts, instruments and calendars. Study them with a sense of setting: what problem was being solved, what method was used, and what evidence the source supports.

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Mathematics grew through practical and scholarly work

The Sulba Sutras preserve geometric procedures for constructing altars and ritual spaces. They show practical measurement and shape construction in a historical setting. The course also names Aryabhata and the Aryabhatiya, Brahmagupta and Bhaskara, alongside arithmetic, geometry, algebra, trigonometry and pi. Pingala's Chandah Sastra is discussed with metre and patterns.

02

Panchanga combines five calendar measures

The five limbs are tithi (lunar day), vara (weekday), nakshatra (lunar mansion), yoga (a calculated angular relationship) and karana (half a tithi). Together these measures organize calendrical information. Define each and show that a Panchanga combines multiple astronomical measures rather than acting as only a list of dates.

03

Instruments make observation concrete

Jantar Mantar observatories used large instruments to observe and calculate astronomical positions. Name the historical context and explain what the instruments were for, then connect design to observation and calculation. Avoid treating a historical instrument as if it answers every modern astronomical question.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Prepare a five-part Panchanga answer.

  1. Write the terms: tithi, vara, nakshatra, yoga and karana.
  2. Add a short meaning after each: lunar day, weekday, lunar mansion, angular relationship and half-tithi.
  3. Finish by saying the framework combines these measures for calendrical decisions.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • Tithi is a lunar day, not simply a numbered day of the modern month.
  • Describe a geometric rule in its setting; do not claim a modern proof unless the source provides one.
  • Keep observation and calculation linked when writing about instruments.

IMPORTANT QUESTIONS · TAP TO REVEAL MODEL ANSWERS
QUESTION 1What is a Panchanga? Name its five parts.+

Panchanga is a traditional Indian calendar framework. Its five limbs are tithi, a lunar day; vara, the weekday; nakshatra, a lunar mansion; yoga, a calculated angular relationship; and karana, half of a tithi. It combines several astronomical measures to support calendrical and ritual decisions. State each term and its role rather than calling it simply a list of dates.

QUESTION 2Describe the contribution of the Sulba Sutras to geometry.+

The Sulba Sutras contain geometric rules and procedures associated with constructing ritual spaces and altars. They show practical geometry through measurement, transformation of shapes and spatial construction. A careful answer situates the material in its historical and ritual setting, explains one example of a geometric procedure, and avoids assuming that a short traditional rule is equivalent to a modern proof unless the specific text supports that comparison.
