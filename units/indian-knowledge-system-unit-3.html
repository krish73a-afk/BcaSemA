Mathematics & astronomy

01

Sulba Sutras and practical geometry

The Sulba Sutras preserve geometric procedures associated with constructing ritual spaces and altars. The material connects measurement, construction and transformation of shapes to a specific historical and ritual setting. Explain the procedure and its purpose as presented in the course. Do not equate a short traditional rule with a modern proof unless the source supports that comparison.

Simple example

A construction problem may require transferring a measured length or making a new altar shape with a prescribed area.

Exam takeawayName the construction context, describe a geometric method and keep claims evidence-based.
02

Mathematicians and mathematical subjects

The guide names Aryabhata and the Aryabhatiya, Brahmagupta and Bhaskara, alongside arithmetic, geometry, algebra, trigonometry and pi. Learn each scholar with the work or topic your class associates with them. A strong historical answer explains the mathematical problem or method, not only a list of famous names.

Simple example

Pair Aryabhata with the Aryabhatiya, then describe the mathematical or astronomical context taught in class.

Exam takeawayUse the course's names, works and examples precisely.
03

Pingala and combinatorial patterns

Pingala's Chandah Sastra connects this unit's mathematical ideas with patterns of syllable lengths in poetic metre. Short and long syllables create combinations that can be counted or ordered. This is a way to study structured patterns in a historical text; explain the connection carefully without claiming it is identical to modern binary computing.

Simple example

With three syllable positions and two choices at each, there are 2×2×2 possible patterns.

Exam takeawayShow the pattern or count and name the prosodic setting.
04

The five parts of a Panchanga

A Panchanga organizes calendrical information through five limbs: tithi (lunar day), vara (weekday), nakshatra (lunar mansion), yoga (a calculated angular relationship) and karana (half a tithi). Each contributes a different time or sky measure. Together they form a traditional calendar framework, rather than simply a list of dates.

Simple example

For a complete answer, write each term followed by its meaning in the same order.

Exam takeawayName all five and define each briefly.
05

Jantar Mantar and astronomical instruments

Jantar Mantar observatories used large instruments for observing and calculating astronomical positions. The instruments linked physical design with observation of the sky and calendrical calculation. Discuss the observatory in its historical setting, name the kind of observation or calculation, and avoid suggesting that a historical instrument answers every question addressed by modern astronomy.

Simple example

An instrument designed around a fixed geometric alignment can help observe a celestial position at a given time.

Exam takeawayConnect the instrument, observation and historical purpose.
06

Sulba geometry and construction

Sulba Sutras describe practical geometric procedures associated with altar construction. Their rules connect measured lengths, shapes and area, showing mathematics applied to a defined task. When studying a historical construction rule, state the context and explain the geometric relationship rather than presenting it as a modern proof unless the syllabus does so.

Simple example

A builder can use measured lengths and a diagonal relationship to construct a right-angled layout.

Exam takeawayName the practical context, describe the construction and show the geometric idea.
07

Pingala's prosody and binary-like patterns

Pingala's work on Sanskrit metre examines patterns made from short and long syllables. A verse pattern can be represented as a sequence of two choices, which creates links to counting and combinatorial thinking. Historical comparison should be careful: explain the pattern the syllabus teaches without claiming that it is identical to modern computer binary arithmetic.

Simple example

For three syllable positions with two choices each, there are 2×2×2=8 possible patterns.

Exam takeawayList patterns in a consistent order and explain the counting rule.
08

Panchanga: five calendar measures

A Panchanga combines tithi, vara, nakshatra, yoga and karana. These describe different calendrical measures, including lunar day, weekday, lunar mansion and calculated relationships. Together they organize time using several astronomical conventions. Memorize the five names with a short meaning for each, and follow the definitions used in your course.

Simple example

A date entry can include both a weekday and a tithi because they describe different measures.

Exam takeawayName and define all five limbs; do not collapse them into a single “date” value.
09

Jantar Mantar and observational instruments

Jantar Mantar observatories contain large instruments designed for observing and calculating astronomical positions and time. Their scale and geometry made certain observations possible, while their accuracy depended on construction and use. Describe a named instrument from class, what it measured and how an observer interpreted the result.

Simple example

An instrument aligned with a celestial direction can help estimate a body's apparent position or local time.

Exam takeawayConnect the instrument's structure to the observation it was designed to support.
10

Place-value calculations in historical mathematics

Place value lets the position of a digit determine its contribution. A zero placeholder preserves empty positions and supports compact written calculations. When explaining a historical method, show a concrete calculation and use the notation in the course. Do not conflate the historical numeral system with later notation without explanation.

Simple example

In 4,072, the zero keeps 4 in the thousands place and 7 in the tens place.

Exam takeawayShow place values and explain the zero's role in the written number.
11

Combinatorics from metre patterns

A sequence of short and long syllables can be treated as choices across positions. With n positions and two independent choices each, there are 2ⁿ patterns. This counting connection makes prosody a useful setting for combinatorial reasoning. Make clear which historical pattern is being discussed and how the modern counting representation is being used.

Simple example

Four syllable positions have 2⁴=16 short/long patterns before any metre rules restrict them.

Exam takeawayDefine the two choices, count the positions and explain any restriction.
12

Calendrical cycles and astronomical calculation

Calendars combine observations and conventions for organizing repeating cycles such as lunar phases, weekdays and seasonal positions. A Panchanga records several measures rather than one simple date. Explain how the measures relate to timekeeping and distinguish observation from the rules used to calculate a calendar entry.

Simple example

A lunar day and a weekday may differ in how their boundaries are calculated.

Exam takeawayDefine each measure and avoid treating calendar terms as interchangeable.
13

Historical instruments and uncertainty

An astronomical instrument turns geometry, alignment and observation into a measured reading. Accuracy depends on construction, calibration, atmospheric conditions and the observer's method. Historical instruments can show sophisticated design while still having limits. Name the measured quantity and describe at least one source of uncertainty.

Simple example

A shadow-based reading can vary with alignment and the exact time of observation.

Exam takeawayExplain instrument principle, output and one practical limitation.
14

Numeration systems and place value

A positional numeral system uses a base and assigns a value to each digit according to its place. In base ten, 3,482 means 3×10³ + 4×10² + 8×10 + 2. Historical numeral traditions developed through use and transmission; explain the mathematical structure rather than attributing every later convention to one moment.

Simple example

In base 5, 234₅ = 2×25 + 3×5 + 4 = 69₁₀.

Exam takeawayExpand each digit by its place value and show the base used.
15

Zero as number and placeholder

Zero can function as a placeholder in positional notation and as a number in arithmetic. These roles are related but conceptually distinct. Historical development involved gradual work across traditions and periods; avoid a simplistic claim that one person invented every use of zero. Explain why zero makes place value compact and unambiguous.

Simple example

205 uses zero to show that no tens are present; 20 and 2 have different values because of place.

Exam takeawayDistinguish placeholder from numerical zero and demonstrate the place-value effect.
16

Arithmetic algorithms and written methods

A written algorithm organizes arithmetic into repeatable steps aligned with place value. Carrying and borrowing are not magic rules: they regroup a quantity into the next place. Checking with an inverse operation or an estimate helps catch errors and makes the reasoning visible.

Simple example

For 352 + 189, 12 ones regroup as 1 ten and 2 ones; then 5 tens + 8 tens + 1 regrouped ten = 14 tens.

Exam takeawayShow regrouping and verify the result with estimation or subtraction.
17

Geometry, measurement and practical mathematics

Geometry and measurement connect mathematical relationships to land, buildings, tools and observation. A formula is meaningful only when its quantities and units match the situation. Historical examples should be tied to a named text or practice, and practical skill should not be confused with a modern formal proof unless the source supports that claim.

Simple example

A rectangular floor 6 m by 4 m has area 24 m², while its perimeter is 20 m; the units differ because area and length measure different things.

Exam takeawayDraw the shape, label units and state whether the question asks for length, area or volume.
18

Patterns and mathematical reasoning

A pattern suggests a rule, but several rules can fit a short sequence. State the rule and test it on more than one term. Mathematical reasoning moves from examples toward a conjecture, then uses a proof or counterexample to establish whether the claim is always true.

Simple example

2, 4, 8, 16 suggests doubling; a finite list alone does not force that rule unless the pattern is stated.

Exam takeawayDefine the rule precisely, test it and distinguish evidence from proof.
19

Mathematical knowledge across languages

Mathematical ideas travel through teaching, translation, trade and commentary. Notation and terminology can change even when an underlying relationship remains recognizable. A careful history credits sources and routes of transmission rather than assigning a complex development to a single culture or individual without evidence.

Simple example

A computation may be expressed in words or verse in one source and later rewritten with symbolic notation.

Exam takeawayDiscuss both the mathematical idea and the context in which the source records it.

How the unit fits together

Connect the definitions, methods and applications before you start memorizing.

Historical mathematics and astronomy can be studied through the questions people were trying to answer. Construction requires measuring and transforming shapes; calendars require organizing observations and time; observatories require instruments that connect geometry with sight lines. The course brings these together through the Sulba Sutras, named mathematicians and works, Panchanga terminology and Jantar Mantar. The best exam answers are specific: name a text or instrument, describe the method or measure, and situate it in its historical purpose. Do not claim a modern theorem or precision unless the source demonstrates that claim. A five-part Panchanga answer is especially suited to a structured response because each limb contributes a distinct calendar measure.

QUESTION 1What is a Panchanga? Name its five parts.+

Panchanga is a traditional Indian calendar framework. Its five limbs are tithi, a lunar day; vara, the weekday; nakshatra, a lunar mansion; yoga, a calculated angular relationship; and karana, half of a tithi. It combines several astronomical measures to support calendrical and ritual decisions. State each term and its role rather than calling it simply a list of dates.

QUESTION 2Describe the contribution of the Sulba Sutras to geometry.+

The Sulba Sutras contain geometric rules and procedures associated with constructing ritual spaces and altars. They show practical geometry through measurement, transformation of shapes and spatial construction. A careful answer situates the material in its historical and ritual setting, explains one example of a geometric procedure, and avoids assuming that a short traditional rule is equivalent to a modern proof unless the specific text supports that comparison.
