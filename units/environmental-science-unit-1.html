🌍 ENVIRONMENTAL SCIENCE & SUSTAINABILITY · UNIT I

Environment, resources & sustainability

The environment is a connected system, and people are part of it. This unit introduces Earth's spheres, the resources we depend on, the effects of using them, and meeting today's needs while protecting future choices.

Earth is a shared home with rooms that connect: air, water, land and living things. If we change one room, the others can feel it.

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Earth's spheres work together

The atmosphere is air; the hydrosphere is water; the lithosphere is solid outer land; and the biosphere is living organisms and their habitats. These are not sealed boxes. Rain moves water through air and land, soil supports plants, and organisms change air and soil. Human settlements, farming and industry interact with all four.

02

Resources have limits and impacts

Natural resources include soil, forests, water, minerals, energy and land. Renewable resources can replenish on a human timescale, but only if use stays within their regeneration rate; a forest can still be depleted by overharvesting. Non-renewable resources form much more slowly than people use them. Dams, mining, deforestation, erosion and land-use change can bring benefits and costs.

03

Sustainability joins needs with fairness

Sustainable choices meet present needs while keeping future generations able to meet theirs. Intergenerational equity considers people in the future. Intragenerational equity asks whether people living today share benefits, costs and access fairly. A groundwater decision, for example, affects today's households and the supply left for future users.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Think through a proposed large dam.

  1. Possible benefits include water storage, electricity and reduced flood risk in some places.
  2. Possible costs include submerged homes, changed river flow and sediment, harm to fish or wetlands, and displacement.
  3. A balanced assessment compares alternatives, listens to affected communities, plans fair rehabilitation, protects ecological flows and monitors results.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • Renewable does not mean unlimited or impact-free.
  • Sustainability includes social wellbeing as well as nature and the economy.
  • Separate fairness among people today from fairness to future generations.

CORE IDEAS TO REMEMBER
01

The atmosphere, hydrosphere, lithosphere and biosphere interact; human society is part of these systems.

02

Natural resources include land, soil, forests, minerals, water and energy.

03

Deforestation, mining, dams, erosion, land-use change and over-extraction can affect ecosystems and communities.

04

Renewable resources replenish on human timescales only when use remains within regeneration limits; non-renewable resources form much more slowly.

05

Intergenerational equity concerns future generations; intragenerational equity concerns fairness among people living today.

IMPORTANT QUESTIONS · TAP TO REVEAL MODEL ANSWERS
QUESTION 1Explain sustainability and the two forms of equity.+

Sustainability means organizing production and consumption so people can meet present needs while maintaining environmental and social conditions for the future. Intergenerational equity asks that current decisions do not unfairly reduce the options of future generations. Intragenerational equity asks whether benefits, costs and access to resources are shared fairly among people today. A strong answer links both ideas to a concrete example such as groundwater use.

QUESTION 2Discuss the environmental and social effects of a large dam.+

A dam can store water, generate electricity and reduce some flood risks. It can also submerge land, alter river flow and sediment movement, affect fish and wetlands, and displace households or tribal communities. Impacts depend on location, design and operation. Planning should assess alternatives, consult affected communities, provide fair resettlement and rehabilitation, protect ecological flows and monitor long-term outcomes.
