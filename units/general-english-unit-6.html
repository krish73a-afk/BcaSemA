Oral communication

Speaking well is more than saying words correctly. Listeners also hear stress, rhythm and intonation, and they notice whether a speaker listens. This unit covers clear conversation and a simple structure for a presentation.

PICTURE IT

Good speaking is not only loud words; it is kind listening and clear signals.

Words are the message; your voice is the road they travel on. Clear pacing and a kind listener help the message reach the other person.

BY THE END, YOU CAN

  • Explain pronunciation, stress, rhythm and intonation.
  • Practise attentive listening and respectful turn-taking.
  • Plan a presentation with a purpose, signposts, examples and a closing.
✧

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Voice features carry meaning

Pronunciation is how a sound or word is produced. Stress makes a syllable or word prominent. Rhythm is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in speech. Intonation is the rise and fall of pitch; it can signal a question, emphasis, contrast or attitude. Speaking clearly does not mean speaking loudly.

02

Listening is active communication

Let the other person finish, show that you are following, and ask a relevant question when something is unclear. Avoid interrupting or preparing your reply so intensely that you miss the point. In a workplace, choose respectful, concise language that suits the listener and situation.

03

Presentations guide an audience

A formal presentation has a purpose and planned route. Open by naming the topic and why it matters, preview a few sections, explain each point with an example, and close with a summary or next step. A casual conversation can change direction more freely, but both forms need clarity and attention to the listener.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Plan a two-minute presentation about a study habit.

  1. Opening: name the habit and why it helps.
  2. Middle: explain two steps and give a practical example for each.
  3. Closing: summarize the idea and invite a question. Speak steadily, pause at section changes and look at the audience.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • Stress means prominence, not simply loudness.
  • Intonation is pitch movement; rhythm is the pattern of strong and weak syllables.
  • A presentation needs a clear audience path, not disconnected facts.

EXAM ANSWER BUILDER

Show your thinking

  1. Start with the key definition or rule.
  2. Show each step, label and example clearly.
  3. Finish with the result and a one-line reason.

If the question says “compare,” write both sides using the same points.

IMPORTANT QUESTIONS · TAP TO REVEAL MODEL ANSWERS
QUESTION 1Explain stress, rhythm and intonation.+

Stress gives prominence to a syllable or word. Rhythm is the pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables in connected speech. Intonation is the pitch movement across a phrase or sentence. Together they help listeners identify focus, contrast and whether a speaker is asking, concluding or expressing uncertainty. Effective communication uses these features naturally and clearly, not by speaking loudly.

QUESTION 2How does a formal presentation differ from a casual conversation?+

A formal presentation has a planned purpose and structure, uses language suited to the audience, and usually has limited time. It opens by stating the topic, develops a small number of organized points with examples, and ends with a summary or action. A casual conversation is more interactive and can shift topics spontaneously. Both require clear speech and attention to the listener.
