🪷 INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM · UNIT I

Introduction to IKS

Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) includes many ways people have organized learning, texts, philosophy, arts and practical knowledge. This unit introduces traditions and categories while reminding us not to flatten different schools into one view.

01

The four Vedas

The four Vedas named in the syllabus are Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda and Atharvaveda. They are foundational bodies of textual tradition and are associated with different collections and forms of material. For this introductory unit, learn the names accurately and use the explanations given in your course rather than assuming all four contain the same kind of text.

Simple example

Write the four names in sequence, then identify them collectively as the Vedas.

Exam takeawayList all four without substituting a philosophical school or Vedanga.
02

The six Vedangas

Vedangas are supporting disciplines associated with studying and transmitting Vedic texts. Shiksha concerns sound and pronunciation; Kalpa ritual procedures; Vyakarana grammar; Nirukta explanation of difficult words; Chandas metre; and Jyotisha timekeeping and calendrical matters. The names are easier to remember when paired with their function.

Simple example

If a question asks how to understand a word, Nirukta is relevant; if it asks about metre, Chandas is relevant.

Exam takeawayName each of the six and pair it with its subject.
03

Philosophical traditions and schools

The guide groups Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva-Mimamsa and Vedanta as Vedic schools, and Charvaka, Buddhist and Jain traditions as non-Vedic. Their views differ on knowledge, reality, self, causation and liberation. Treat the grouping as the syllabus presents it, then distinguish schools by their questions rather than describing one unified viewpoint.

Simple example

Nyaya is associated with reasoning and means of knowledge; Yoga discusses disciplined practice and liberation within its own tradition.

Exam takeawayState the course grouping, then give one careful distinguishing concern.
04

Itihasa, Puranic and niti literature

In this course, Itihasa commonly refers to the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Puranic literature and niti literature are distinct genres with different ways of presenting stories, traditions, conduct or guidance. Their purposes should not be collapsed into one category. When you discuss a text, name its genre and use the context taught in class.

Simple example

An answer can distinguish an Itihasa narrative from niti material concerned with conduct or practical guidance.

Exam takeawayName the genre and explain its role instead of listing texts alone.
05

Caturdasa Vidyasthana and 64 Kalas

Caturdasa Vidyasthana and the 64 Kalas are syllabus concepts for classifying bodies of learning and arts. The precise contents and arrangement should follow the course's list, since traditions and sources can organize these categories differently. Use them to show that knowledge was discussed through named disciplines and arts, not as one undivided subject.

Simple example

Organize your class list into its named learning areas or arts before memorizing individual examples.

Exam takeawayUse the exact classifications and examples included in your course notes.
06

Oral, written and teacher–student transmission

Knowledge can be preserved and shared through oral recitation, written texts, teaching lineages, debate and practical training. Each method has strengths and limits: memorized recitation can preserve wording, while a manuscript can be copied, annotated or damaged. Studying how knowledge travelled helps explain why texts, teachers and institutions all matter in a knowledge tradition.

Simple example

A learner may study a text with a teacher, practise its recitation and discuss how a passage should be interpreted.

Exam takeawayName the method of transmission and explain what it contributes.
07

The six Vedangas as supporting disciplines

The Vedangas support the study and use of Vedic texts: Shiksha concerns sound, Kalpa procedures, Vyakarana grammar, Nirukta word explanation, Chandas metre and Jyotisha calendrical timing. They are complementary tools rather than six versions of the same subject. Linking each name to its task makes the list easier to remember and explain.

Simple example

Chandas helps identify a verse's metre; Shiksha guides its pronunciation.

Exam takeawayWrite all six names and pair each with its distinct purpose.
08

Darshanas and different philosophical questions

The syllabus names six Vedic schools—Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Mimamsa and Vedanta—and non-Vedic traditions such as Charvaka, Buddhist and Jain thought. They do not all make the same claims. Compare them through questions such as what counts as knowledge, what is real, how action works and what liberation means.

Simple example

Nyaya emphasizes reasoning and sources of knowledge; Yoga sets out a disciplined path of practice and insight.

Exam takeawayName the traditions accurately and compare one question they answer differently.
09

Itihasa, Purana and niti are distinct genres

Itihasa, Puranic literature and niti literature have different purposes and forms. The Ramayana and Mahabharata are commonly treated as Itihasa in the course; Puranas include narrative and cosmological material; niti texts discuss conduct, counsel or practical wisdom. Treat genre as a clue to how a text organizes and communicates knowledge.

Simple example

A story may illustrate a question of duty, while a niti passage may state a practical principle more directly.

Exam takeawayDefine each genre in the course's terms and give a named example.

How the unit fits together

Connect the definitions, methods and applications before you start memorizing.

The unit is an introduction to a wide family of traditions, not a claim that every tradition shares one philosophy. Start by learning the organizing vocabulary: textual bodies, supporting disciplines, philosophical schools, genres and classifications of arts or knowledge. Then explain the role each category plays. The Vedangas are tools for textual study; philosophical schools debate questions about knowledge, reality and liberation; narrative and niti literature have different purposes. The course's grouping of Vedic and non-Vedic schools should be presented as its framework. When an exam asks for comparison, name the traditions and give one distinctive question or method instead of compressing them into a single viewpoint.

QUESTION 1What are the Vedas and Vedangas?+

The four Vedas are Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda and Atharvaveda. The Vedangas are six supporting disciplines: Shiksha concerns sound and pronunciation; Kalpa concerns ritual procedures; Vyakarana concerns grammar; Nirukta explains difficult words; Chandas studies metre; and Jyotisha concerns timekeeping and calendrical matters. Together they show how textual knowledge was studied through different specialized methods.

QUESTION 2Briefly distinguish Vedic and non-Vedic philosophical schools.+

The syllabus groups Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva-Mimamsa and Vedanta as Vedic schools, while Charvaka, Buddhist and Jain traditions are presented as non-Vedic. Their views differ on knowledge, reality, self, causation and liberation. A strong exam answer names the schools and gives one central concern of each instead of suggesting that all Indian philosophy has one single position.
