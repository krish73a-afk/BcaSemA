🪷 INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM · UNIT I

Introduction to IKS

Indian Knowledge Systems (IKS) includes many ways people have organized learning, texts, philosophy, arts and practical knowledge. This unit introduces traditions and categories while reminding us not to flatten different schools into one view.

PICTURE IT

A library has many shelves. IKS explores different shelves of learning and ways to organize them.

Think of a large library with different shelves and methods. The shelves connect, but a grammar book, a poem and a philosophy text do different jobs.

  • Recognize the philosophical traditions named in the syllabus.

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Texts and supporting disciplines

The four Vedas are Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda and Atharvaveda. The six Vedangas support their study: Shiksha concerns sound, Kalpa procedures, Vyakarana grammar, Nirukta word explanation, Chandas metre and Jyotisha timekeeping or calendrical matters. This shows that learning used specialized tools for preserving and interpreting texts.

02

Philosophical schools ask different questions

The syllabus groups Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva-Mimamsa and Vedanta as Vedic schools, and Charvaka, Buddhist and Jain traditions as non-Vedic. These traditions differ on knowledge, reality, self, causation and liberation. State the grouping used in the course and give a central concern instead of implying all thought agrees.

03

Genres and classifications have their purposes

Itihasa commonly refers here to the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Puranic and niti literature have distinct genres and aims. Caturdasa Vidyasthana and the 64 Kalas are course concepts for classifying learning and arts; explain them using the terms and organization in your class notes.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Build a short answer about the Vedangas.

  1. Name all six: Shiksha, Kalpa, Vyakarana, Nirukta, Chandas and Jyotisha.
  2. Pair each with its role: sound, procedures, grammar, difficult words, metre and timekeeping.
  3. Close by explaining that these disciplines support careful study and transmission of knowledge.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • Do not mix a Vedanga's name with another discipline's purpose.
  • Do not describe every philosophical school as holding the same view.
  • Use the course's definitions for the 14 knowledge stations and 64 arts.

QUESTION 1What are the Vedas and Vedangas?+

The four Vedas are Rigveda, Yajurveda, Samaveda and Atharvaveda. The Vedangas are six supporting disciplines: Shiksha concerns sound and pronunciation; Kalpa concerns ritual procedures; Vyakarana concerns grammar; Nirukta explains difficult words; Chandas studies metre; and Jyotisha concerns timekeeping and calendrical matters. Together they show how textual knowledge was studied through different specialized methods.

QUESTION 2Briefly distinguish Vedic and non-Vedic philosophical schools.+

The syllabus groups Samkhya, Yoga, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Purva-Mimamsa and Vedanta as Vedic schools, while Charvaka, Buddhist and Jain traditions are presented as non-Vedic. Their views differ on knowledge, reality, self, causation and liberation. A strong exam answer names the schools and gives one central concern of each instead of suggesting that all Indian philosophy has one single position.
