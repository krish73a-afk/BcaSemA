🪷 INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM · UNIT IV

Science, craft & technology

Technical knowledge is visible in the materials, buildings and art people made. This unit explores metals, dyes, painting, town planning and architecture, with attention to craft processes and regional context.

PICTURE IT

Making a useful object takes careful choices: pick a material, shape it, test it, and improve it.

A craftsperson is a careful observer: choose a material, understand how it behaves, shape it, finish it, then use it for a real purpose.

BY THE END, YOU CAN

  • Describe metalworking through materials and process steps.
  • Connect dyes, painting and architecture with craft knowledge.
  • Discuss Vastu Shastra and temple forms with historical and regional care.

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Metalworking is a chain of decisions

An answer can identify a metal and its use, then describe ore selection, heating, smelting, shaping, joining and finishing. The syllabus names copper, gold, zinc, mercury, lead and silver, as well as iron and steel history. Tools, furnaces, alloying and trade connect technical work to society.

02

Dyes and painting join material with technique

Color on cloth or a wall depends on materials and processes: prepare pigment or dye, apply it to a surface, and use methods that help it remain visible. The course places these arts within technical and occupational knowledge. Use specific class examples and explain the process rather than making broad claims.

03

Built spaces express design and local traditions

Town planning and temple architecture involve layout, materials, structure and site. Many temple traditions include a sanctum, a tower or superstructure, halls, gateways and sculpture, but regional forms differ. Vastu Shastra is a body of architectural principles and texts; describe its history without presenting every claim as modern engineering evidence.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Structure an answer about iron or steel.

  1. Name the material and a documented use, time or region if your source gives one.
  2. Describe supported steps: prepare the material, heat or smelt it, shape it and finish it.
  3. Connect the technique with artisans, tools, trade or social needs; avoid unsupported claims of universal superiority.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • Name a time, region or source when known; do not generalize across periods.
  • Do not describe all temples as having an identical plan.
  • A historical design principle is not automatically proof of a modern engineering claim.

EXAM ANSWER BUILDER

Show your thinking

  1. Start with the key definition or rule.
  2. Show each step, label and example clearly.
  3. Finish with the result and a one-line reason.

If the question says “compare,” write both sides using the same points.

CORE IDEAS TO REMEMBER
01

Discuss metals such as copper, gold, zinc, mercury, lead and silver through their historical uses and production methods.

02

Iron and steel history can include furnaces, alloying practices, tools and trade; distinguish documented techniques from broad claims.

03

Dyes and painting technologies combine material knowledge with craft practice.

04

Town planning and temple architecture involve layout, materials, structural forms and local traditions.

05

Vastu Shastra is a body of architectural principles and texts; describe its historical concepts without presenting every claim as modern engineering evidence.

IMPORTANT QUESTIONS · TAP TO REVEAL MODEL ANSWERS
QUESTION 1How can ancient Indian metalworking be discussed in an exam answer?+

Start by naming the material and its use, then describe the knowledge involved: ore selection, heating, smelting, shaping, joining or finishing. Mention iron and steel, copper, zinc or other metals covered in class. Connect the work to artisans, trade and social needs. Avoid unsupported statements about universal superiority; a careful answer gives the example, time or region when the source is known.

QUESTION 2Describe major elements of Indian temple architecture.+

A temple is both a sacred space and a designed structure. Common elements discussed in many traditions include a sanctum, a tower or superstructure, halls, gateways, sculpture and a planned relationship to the surrounding site. Regional forms vary, so identify the style or region when known. Explain how layout, symbolism, materials and craft work together rather than describing all temples as identical.
