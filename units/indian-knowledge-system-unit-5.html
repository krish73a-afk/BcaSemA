🪷 INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM · UNIT V

Humanities & society

This unit brings knowledge into everyday and public life: food, sleep, health, yoga, psychology, ethics, administration and governance. Explain concepts within their traditions and distinguish historical texts from today's institutions.

PICTURE IT

People need food, rest, care, rules, and ways to live together. Knowledge includes all those questions.

A community needs personal wellbeing and fair ways to make shared decisions. Knowledge can ask how to care for oneself and how people organize life together.

BY THE END, YOU CAN

  • Explain the course's treatment of daily habits, yoga and wellbeing.
  • Describe sattva, rajas and tamas as a traditional framework.
  • Discuss the Ramayana and Arthashastra with historical context.
✧

LESSON PATH

Build the idea step by step

Read one card, say it back in your own words, then try the example.

01

Wellbeing includes daily habits and traditions

The syllabus considers food, sleep, water and daily practice in Ayurveda and yoga traditions. Yoga includes practices and philosophies, so name the path or practice instead of reducing it to posture. Say what the course source describes and avoid presenting a historical framework as a medical diagnosis.

02

Triguna is a way of describing qualities

Sattva is associated with clarity and balance, rajas with activity and desire, and tamas with inertia or obscurity. This is a conceptual framework in particular traditions, not a measured modern personality test. Explain the terms with an example and keep their context clear.

03

Texts offer perspectives on governance and ethics

The Ramayana and Arthashastra provide different perspectives on duty, governance and statecraft. The Arthashastra can be discussed through rulers, officials, revenue, law, diplomacy and security. Explain the setting and purpose; do not equate prescriptions directly with present-day constitutional or democratic rules.

✦

WORK IT THROUGH

Compare a classical governance text with a modern institution carefully.

  1. Name the text and one issue it discusses, such as administration or revenue.
  2. Describe the historical idea before comparing it with a present-day question.
  3. State the difference in context and institutions; use the comparison to clarify rather than claim the systems are identical.

WATCH OUT FOR

Easy mix-ups

  • Yoga is broader than physical postures in the course's framing.
  • Triguna is a traditional conceptual framework, not a clinical measure.
  • A historical text's prescriptions are not current constitutional rules.

IMPORTANT QUESTIONS · TAP TO REVEAL MODEL ANSWERS
QUESTION 1Explain the Triguna framework in brief.+

The Triguna framework describes three qualities: sattva is associated with clarity and balance; rajas with activity and desire; and tamas with inertia or obscurity. In the syllabus it supports an Indian approach to psychology and wellbeing. A sound answer explains the terms as a conceptual framework within particular traditions, not as measured modern personality categories.

QUESTION 2How can the Arthashastra be used to discuss governance?+

The Arthashastra is a classical text associated with statecraft and administration. It can be discussed through its treatment of the ruler, officials, revenue, law, diplomacy and security. Explain the text's historical setting and its practical orientation. Avoid equating its prescriptions directly with contemporary democratic institutions; instead compare its questions about public order and administration with modern governance carefully.
