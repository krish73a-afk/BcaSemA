🪷 INDIAN KNOWLEDGE SYSTEM · UNIT V

Humanities & society

This unit brings knowledge into everyday and public life: food, sleep, health, yoga, psychology, ethics, administration and governance. Explain concepts within their traditions and distinguish historical texts from today's institutions.

PICTURE IT

People need food, rest, care, rules, and ways to live together. Knowledge includes all those questions.

A community needs personal wellbeing and fair ways to make shared decisions. Knowledge can ask how to care for oneself and how people organize life together.

BY THE END, YOU CAN

  • Explain the course's treatment of daily habits, yoga and wellbeing.
  • Describe sattva, rajas and tamas as a traditional framework.
  • Discuss the Ramayana and Arthashastra with historical context.

01

Food, water, sleep and wellbeing

The unit considers how daily habits such as food, water, rest and routine relate to health in Ayurveda and yoga traditions. Explain the framework or practice as the course presents it and give the historical or conceptual context. Do not turn a short syllabus idea into individualized medical advice or treat all traditions as identical.

Simple example

A course answer can describe why routine and balanced habits are emphasized, then name the tradition being discussed.

Exam takeawayIdentify the framework and explain the idea without making unsupported medical claims.
02

Yoga includes practices and philosophies

Yoga is not only posture. The syllabus presents it as a set of practices and philosophies that can include discipline, attention, ethical conduct and paths toward liberation. A good answer names the specific path or practice being discussed and explains its purpose in that tradition, rather than using the word yoga as if it referred to only one exercise.

Simple example

If discussing meditation, state the practice and its role rather than describing it as a posture.

Exam takeawayName the path or practice and use the course's context.
03

Triguna: sattva, rajas and tamas

The Triguna framework describes qualities: sattva is associated with clarity and balance, rajas with activity and desire, and tamas with inertia or obscurity. These ideas are used within particular philosophical traditions to discuss mind and nature. They are a conceptual framework, not measured modern personality categories or clinical diagnoses.

Simple example

A focused, calm state can illustrate sattva; restless activity can illustrate rajas; heaviness or inertia can illustrate tamas.

Exam takeawayDefine all three and identify the framework as a traditional concept.
04

Ethics, duty and the Ramayana

The Ramayana is discussed in the course as a text that can offer perspectives on duty, conduct and social roles. Interpret examples in the story's literary and historical setting. A character's action can prompt ethical discussion, but a single episode should not be treated as a universal rule without explaining context and the interpretive lens used.

Simple example

An answer can describe a duty conflict in a narrative, then explain the values it raises.

Exam takeawayUse a named episode or idea and explain its context before interpreting it.
05

Arthashastra and statecraft

The Arthashastra is a classical text associated with administration and statecraft. The course points to topics such as rulers, officials, revenue, law, diplomacy and security. A careful answer explains the text's practical orientation and historical setting. It should not equate its prescriptions directly with present-day constitutional or democratic institutions.

Simple example

Discuss revenue administration as one topic, then distinguish the text's setting from a current public finance system.

Exam takeawayName a governance topic and keep historical prescription separate from current law.
06

Historical knowledge and present-day institutions

When comparing an older text or framework with modern society, first describe the historical idea accurately. Then state the point of comparison and the important difference in context, values or institutions. This avoids treating past and present as interchangeable and helps an answer show both understanding and judgment.

Simple example

Compare a classical account of administration with a modern institution by naming one shared question and one structural difference.

Exam takeawayUse context, comparison and limitation in that order.
07

Daily routines, food, water and sleep

The syllabus discusses wellbeing through habits such as food, water, sleep and daily routine. Describe a traditional framework in its own terms and separate it from present-day clinical advice. For a student, a concrete learning point is that regular rest, hydration and balanced routines support attention and consistent study, while individual health needs differ.

Simple example

A study plan that includes sleep and breaks is more sustainable than an all-night revision schedule.

Exam takeawayExplain the framework respectfully and use careful, evidence-aware wording.
08

Yoga paths and practices

Yoga includes philosophical ideas and several kinds of practice, not only physical postures. Depending on the syllabus, distinguish ethical discipline, meditation, knowledge, action or devotion from physical techniques. Explain the practice's purpose within its tradition and avoid treating one modern class format as the entire subject.

Simple example

A breathing or meditation practice can be discussed as attention training within a wider path of disciplined practice.

Exam takeawayName the practice, its context and how it relates to the broader idea of yoga.
09

Triguna as a contextual framework

Triguna describes three qualities: sattva is associated with clarity or balance, rajas with activity or desire, and tamas with inertia or obscurity. The framework is used in particular Indian philosophical and cultural traditions. It should not be treated as a measured modern diagnosis or a fixed label for a person's character.

Simple example

A passage describing calm clarity may be interpreted through sattva, while a restless desire for action may be discussed through rajas.

Exam takeawayDefine the three terms and state the tradition or context in which they are used.
10

Dharma, Arthashastra and governance

The Ramayana can be studied for narratives about duty and ethical choice; the Arthashastra discusses statecraft through administration, revenue, law, diplomacy and security. They are different texts with different aims. Historical prescriptions should be explained in context rather than equated directly with present constitutional systems or democratic institutions.

Simple example

Compare a narrative ethical dilemma with a practical administrative rule and identify the different purpose of each text.

Exam takeawayName the text, its concern and one contextual limitation of a modern comparison.

How the unit fits together

Connect the definitions, methods and applications before you start memorizing.

This unit connects personal wellbeing with ideas about psychology, ethics and public administration. Keep each framework in its own context. Ayurveda and yoga traditions discuss daily practices such as food, water and rest; the course may describe these as historical or philosophical approaches, not personal medical instructions. Triguna offers a vocabulary for qualities of mind and nature. The Ramayana and Arthashastra offer different textual perspectives on duty and governance. A strong comparative answer first describes a text's own concerns and historical setting, then identifies a point of comparison with modern institutions. This prevents an anachronism: a classical prescription and a current constitution answer questions in different social and political contexts.

EXAMPLE 1

Apply the Triguna terms to a short illustration

TaskA person feels calm and clear, then later becomes restless and driven. Use the framework carefully.

  1. Use sattva for the state associated with clarity and balance.
  2. Use rajas for activity and desire; explain the change in state.
  3. If discussing heaviness or inertia, use tamas with its course definition.
  4. Say these are traditional conceptual qualities, not a clinical diagnosis.
Answer and reason

The example demonstrates vocabulary while preserving the framework's limits.

EXAMPLE 2

Compare classical and modern governance

TaskUse Arthashastra as a historical source and compare one administrative question.

  1. Name the Arthashastra and its association with statecraft.
  2. Describe a specific topic such as officials, revenue, law or diplomacy.
  3. Name the modern question being compared, such as public revenue or administration.
  4. Explain one similarity in the problem and one difference in institutions or context.
Answer and reason

A careful comparison does not treat the text as a current constitutional rulebook.

QUESTION 1Explain the Triguna framework in brief.+

The Triguna framework describes three qualities: sattva is associated with clarity and balance; rajas with activity and desire; and tamas with inertia or obscurity. In the syllabus it supports an Indian approach to psychology and wellbeing. A sound answer explains the terms as a conceptual framework within particular traditions, not as measured modern personality categories.

QUESTION 2How can the Arthashastra be used to discuss governance?+

The Arthashastra is a classical text associated with statecraft and administration. It can be discussed through its treatment of the ruler, officials, revenue, law, diplomacy and security. Explain the text's historical setting and its practical orientation. Avoid equating its prescriptions directly with contemporary democratic institutions; instead compare its questions about public order and administration with modern governance carefully.
